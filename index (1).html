SCRAPP-E

Ecologically Sustainable Autonomous Companion & Disability Assistance Robot

A modular robot made from recycled parts by UNT students. It captions speech, describes what it sees, fetches objects, and keeps you company, so people with hearing, vision, and mobility challenges can live more independently.

About SCRAPP-E

The problem

People with disabilities often can't find assistive technology that is affordable, adaptable, and friendly. Existing options tend to be costly, do one job, and are hard to change as needs change.

Our answer

A low-cost, modular robot. Attachments can be swapped or upgraded without replacing the whole machine, so each robot fits its user and less material is wasted.

What it will do

  • Speech-to-text captions on its LCD face
  • Audible descriptions of what it sees
  • Voice-activated object retrieval
  • Language translation
  • Companionship

Built to last, built from leftovers

Most parts come from disassembled 3D printers and the robotics room: NEMA 17 motors, aluminum extrusions, power supplies, fans, LCD screens, and belts. New parts are a last resort.

  • Battery and panels come off by hand in under two minutes
  • Handles carpet, tile, hardwood, and vinyl
  • Smooth edges and slow exposed joints, following ISO 13482 safety thinking

By April 1, 2027

A working chassis and drivetrain, a sensor system that spots obstacles, and autonomous forward and backward movement. The test: drive 500 ft in a straight line with no help, and stop before hitting an obstacle at least 2 ft tall. The head, arm, and advanced autonomy come in later phases, and the modular design lets future student teams pick them up.
